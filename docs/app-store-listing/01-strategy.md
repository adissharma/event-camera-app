# App Store product page strategy

Researched on 3 November 2026 from the US App Store results for "disposable photo event".
Updated on 7 November to follow the website as it now stands on stills.events, after PR #23,
and the refreshed app design. Stills is still on version 1.0, with no ratings in the US and one
in the UK.

## The short version

Everyone in this category sells the same mechanism, in nearly the same black-and-serif
wrapper: QR code, no download, shared album, delayed reveal.

The website now makes three calls that the App Store page should copy:

- **Say what it is.** Nobody has heard of Stills, so every headline names the thing plainly. A
  stranger should be able to repeat it back.
- **Disposable is a headline feature, not a mode.** "Turn every phone into a disposable camera" now
  has its own scene. It's also the word people search for, so the page should land it early.
- **Every guest is the photographer.** The value is the moments the host's photographer can't
  catch, rather than a feature list.

The words explain, and the pictures excite: hot pink and violet woodcut with real colour
photographs bursting out of it, which no rival looks like. The warmth sits in the supporting
lines: your mum in tears, your best friend at 1am.

> The shared camera app for weddings and parties. → Your guests are already taking the photos.

### Patterns that matter

1. **The four core claims are table stakes.** QR, no download, one album and a reveal appear on
   nearly every listing, so they get a host to "yes, this is the thing" but don't win anything.
2. **The plainest copy is also the best.** Lense's literal headlines are the clearest in the
   category. Where rivals go wrong is naming small features (film filters, Live Activities,
   your rules) rather than being too direct. Plain copy has to name something the host wants.
3. **Dark, centred and serif is now the default.** Once, Scene, Reveal and Stills' current
   listing could almost be swapped for each other. Hot pink stands out on sight.
4. **The leaders sell on proof.** POV and Lense put ratings on the first frame. We have none
   yet, so the first frames must do the work with product and emotion instead.
5. **Almost nobody mentions price in the set**, although every listing shows "In-App Purchases".

## Who is deciding, and what they're asking

The buyer is the **host**, not the guest. Usually they're planning a wedding or a big
birthday, and they aren't comparing many apps. They give the page a few seconds and
ask these questions, in this order:

1. What is this, and is it what I searched for?
2. Will my guests actually use it, including my nan?
3. What do I end up with?
4. Is it worth it, and what's the catch?
5. Does it suit my kind of event?

## The strategy

**1. Say what it is, straight away.** Many people arrive cold, and with no ratings there's
nothing else to reassure them. Frame 1 is a plain description that someone could repeat to
their partner.

**2. Make the first three frames a complete pitch.** Search results can show just the first
few screenshots, and a lot of people never swipe. (This comes from ASO sources, as Apple doesn't
document it.) What it is, how guests join, and the disposable camera people searched for.

**3. Land "disposable" in the first three.** Most of this category's searches use the word, and the
subtitle will say it. Someone who searched for a disposable camera app should see one before they
swipe. The frame also carries the payoff: photos that develop overnight.

**4. Make every feature earn its frame by answering a worry.** Photo challenges aren't there because
rivals lack them. They're there because hosts are afraid guests won't take part.

**5. Let the look do the exciting.** Plain words in a plain wrapper would blend straight into the
category. Hot pink and violet, one big left-aligned headline, a large phone that runs off the edge,
and real photographs flying out of it.

**6. Tell one night.** The light moves from sunset to night to sunrise across the set, as on the
website, so swiping feels like living through the party.

**7. Replace missing proof with honesty and specifics.** No invented ratings, quotes, laurels or
guest counts. Real product screens and named guests do the convincing until real reviews arrive.

**Where this differs from the website:**

- **Disposable comes third, not seventh.** The site can save it, because scrolling carries people
  there. Here it has to be in the frames most people see.
- **Seating moves from second to eighth.** It's useful, but it's not why someone downloads a
  shared camera. It's also the frame at risk if the floor plan isn't in the live build.
- **Frame 7 drops "video and message".** Those are Stills+ only, and a tag would weaken the payoff.
  Photos are on every plan, so it can stay untagged.
- **Frame 9 says "Group trips" rather than "Holidays".** On the US store, "holidays" reads as Christmas.

**If you want fewer:** 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and 9 tell the whole story. Drop 8 first if the floor plan
isn't in the live build yet, then 6.

## Wording and rules to stay within Apple's guidelines

- **Mark paid features.** Guideline 2.3.2 says screenshots must show when featured items need
  an extra purchase. Challenges, the guestbook and video are Stills+ only, so frames 5 and 6
  carry a small "with Stills+" tag. Frame 7 says photos only, so it needs no tag.
- **No prices on screenshots.** Guideline 2.3.14 rules out specific pricing in creative
  assets. Frame 9 says "Pay once per event. No subscription." The "free for up to 5 guests"
  line goes in the promotional text instead.
- **Show the app in use.** Guideline 2.3.3 says screenshots should show the app in use, and every
  frame here has a real screen in it.
- **Don't name other platforms.** Guideline 2.3.10 applies, so no mention of Android, even
  though guests on other phones can join in a browser.
- **No awards, Apple logos or Apple-endorsement cues.** Guideline 5.2.4 rules these out, and it's
  what ChatGPT tends to add when it imitates App Store art.

## Fix alongside the new screenshots

The listing as it stands today has these problems:

1. **Screenshots 3 and 4 are the same image.** Both are the "Only on Stills" file.
2. **The name doesn't match how you now describe the app.** "Event Camera & Games" doesn't say shared
   camera, and "Games" suggests the wrong kind of app.
3. **The description doesn't mention challenges, the guestbook or seating**, which are your best
   arguments, and it doesn't say that guests don't need to download anything.
4. **The upload size.** The current images are 1242 × 2688. Upload 1320 × 2868 to the 6.9" slot and
   1206 × 2622 to the 6.1" or 6.3" slot. The sizing step in the prompts file makes both.
5. **Don't upload `assets/app-store/share-event-6.9.png`.** It's a copy of a screenshot from Once,
   and shows "Together, Once" on the phone.

### Why the name changes again

My first suggestion was "Stills: Disposable Camera", because every app in the results had
"Disposable" in its name. That evidence was weak, because I'd searched for "disposable". So I ran
nine more searches through Apple's public search feed:

| Search | Apps named "Disposable" (of about 24) | Who leads | Top ratings |
|---|---|---|---|
| event camera | 14 | POV, Once, Lense | 15.6K, 713, 1.5K |
| party camera | 12 | Party!, POV, Party Cam | 232, 15.6K, 68 |
| shared camera | 3 | Party!, Disposable Life, Dump, Ours, the roll | 232, 1, 13, 2, 0 |
| wedding guest photos | 3 | POV, WeddingWire, AllShots | 15.6K, 2K, 29 |

**"Shared camera" is the search Stills could actually win**, because the apps around it have almost
no ratings. It's also what your website calls the product. "Disposable" belongs in the subtitle, since
people clearly search for it and the website now gives it its own headline.

Two caveats: Apple publishes no search volumes, so "disposable" may well be searched far more, and
this feed isn't identical to the App Store's own search. Stills didn't appear in any of the nine,
even for "event camera", which may be because it's two days old.

## After launch

- **Test the old hook.** Once there's enough traffic, run Apple's Product Page Optimization on frame 1:
  the plain headline against "You'll miss most of your own party." Going plain is a judgment call,
  not measured data, and on a page that's already explained itself, the emotional line may well
  win. Low traffic will make early results slow to reach significance.
- **Make custom product pages for ads.** Reviews suggest Instagram ads are a real source, so give
  each ad its own page. A wedding page could open on "The shared camera app for weddings", with
  seating and the guestbook moved up. Birthday and bach-party pages can use their own settings
  from the web page.
- **Put the first real rating on frame 1 when it's earned.** Leaders rely on this, and a real
  number beats anything else here.
- **Rating prompt at the reveal.** Built on 4 November, and it ships with the next version. It asks
  hosts on their revealed gallery, and that's the fastest way to get the proof the leaders have.
